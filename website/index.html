Media in. Markdown out.

Local Transcriber turns audio, video, YouTube links and your own recordings into timestamped Markdown, with speech recognition on your Mac. Read the results in the app and play the recording from any timestamp.

One folder, one file per source.

Drop something in, say which language is spoken, and it starts. Each finished transcript is written straight to a folder you chose once.

  1. 1

    Add media

    Drag files or YouTube links onto the window, click to choose files, or copy and press ⌘V. Or press Record to capture your microphone, one app’s audio, or both. Closing the window or quitting while you record asks first.

    MP4 MOV M4A MP3 WAV, youtube.com, youtu.be

  2. 2

    Transcribe

    Choose the language spoken in each file, link or recording; your three most recent languages come first. One job runs at a time, Processing in the sidebar shows its progress while you keep working in the Library, and the finished transcript opens when you are done.

  3. 3

    On your Mac

    Apple's speech stack does the recognition locally. For video, the audio is extracted first.

  4. 4

    Save

    Timestamped Markdown lands in your output folder, which you change from the footer. Existing files are never overwritten.

The transcript is the artifact.

One Markdown file per source, ordered by audio time and grouped by minute. The app does not summarize, index, rewrite or "clean up" what was said.

YouTube links can instead be saved as a source package: the audio, a small source.json and the transcript, together in one folder named after the video ID.

For Obsidian and other Markdown tools, Settings can start new transcripts with a YAML block: source, language, transcriber and duration. It is off by default.

Read it back, and listen.

A sidebar lists the transcripts in your output folder: Pinned, your own folders, Recent and Archive. Rename, pin, archive or move to the Trash from the context menu, or drag a transcript onto a folder to move it. A new transcript carries a small New badge until you open it. The Markdown file stays the source of truth.

The library is a scan of the folder you selected and the folders directly inside it. Nothing is imported and no database is created.

The app follows the Mac's appearance, or stays Light or Dark if you choose that in Settings.

  • Copy puts the whole Markdown on the clipboard, without a YAML block at the top.
  • When the recording is saved next to its transcript, a player appears. Click a timestamp or any timed line to jump there and play.
  • The current line is highlighted and the view follows it. Jump to Playing brings you back if you scroll away.
  • Each new transcript gets a Name.timing.json beside its Markdown, with the recognizer's timing.

Transcription stays on your Mac.

A YouTube link needs the network to fetch its audio. That download is temporary and deleted when the job ends. Recording and transcription happen on the Mac.

An early release, with known limits.

Processing, languages, output, source packages, recording, the library and playback work and were checked in the real app. Some edges are known.

Found another one, or have an idea? Email info@wavesweb.nl or open an issue on GitHub.

  • YouTube is the only link source: no other sites, playlists or channels.
  • Downloads use a pinned yt-dlp that never updates itself, so a change on YouTube's side can break them.
  • A single download is limited to 30 minutes.
  • Playback needs the audio in the same folder as the transcript. Audio imported from elsewhere, and YouTube transcripts saved without their audio, open for reading only.
  • Transcripts without a valid timing file, such as those made before 0.6, open as Markdown without clickable timing.
  • Jump positions follow the recognizer's timing and are not guaranteed to be exact. Dutch Dictation output can be sparse.
  • Apple lets the app keep only a few languages prepared. When a new one needs room, the least recently used is released and may have to be downloaded again.
