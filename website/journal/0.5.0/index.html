0.5.0
Oct 7–8, 2026
GitHub Release

The same tool, finished

0.5.0 can do everything 0.4.0 could, and nothing more. What changed is how that work is presented and operated: how the window is arranged, how recording is set up and how the app speaks. It now belongs to the same family as this site, and it still behaves like a Mac app.

Why a small utility needs an identity

Until 0.4 the window grew one release at a time: a drop target, an Add Files button, a Record button that opened a popover, a Clear button that disappeared while the queue ran. Each piece was decided when its feature arrived, and each did its job. What the window never had was a pass over the whole, and next to the site it came from, with its warm paper, wine accent and seven-bar mark, it looked like a different product.

A tool I use every day deserved the same care in how it looks as in how it works. That didn't need new features. It needed the existing ones arranged, colored and worded together.

Wavesweb DNA, native Mac grammar

That was the rule for the whole release. Wavesweb supplies the character: the palette, the serif accent, the spacing, the mark and the way the app talks. macOS keeps the grammar: controls, keyboard and focus, windows and accessibility. The goal was never to turn a SwiftUI app into a web page.

Before any app code changed, the direction was written down as a contract and tried in throwaway SwiftUI prototypes at the 640 × 420 minimum window, in light, dark and Increase Contrast. Then it was applied. The window ground is the site's paper color in light and dark, continued under the title bar. The empty window is one surface with two zones: drop or choose media on the left, record on the right. The Record popover is gone, so the source you are about to record is always visible. Once there are jobs, Add Files, recording and Transcribe share one action bar, and Clear Queue lives in the queue header, disabled while a job runs instead of vanishing.

The voice changed with it. Messages say what happened, then what to do, and keep every fact. “Local Transcriber may not use the microphone. Allow it in …” became “Local Transcriber isn’t allowed to use the microphone. Turn it on in System Settings › Privacy & Security › Microphone.”

What I left alone

Wine appears in two places: the fill of the main button and the checkboxes. Focus rings, pickers and menus keep the accent color you chose for your Mac. An accent only means something when it is rare. On the main action it says where to press; spread over pickers, menus and text it would compete with the system's own signals and be harder to read in dark mode.

Drag and drop, ⌘V, the file panels, the Options menu, Finder reveal and the window chrome are the system’s, unchanged. Light and dark follow macOS; there is no theme switch and no accent picker. The mark never moves, not even while recording, because a moving wave would pretend to be a level meter. The app icon is the same.

Staying native has a price I accepted. The app picker is a standard menu, so a long app name is cut off at the end rather than in the middle, as the design asked. A custom control would fix that and lose the native behavior; the full name is in the menu and its tooltip.

Nothing new to do

The queue, the recording sources, the permissions and the timestamped Markdown are the same as in 0.4.0; what changed is how you reach and read them. Keeping it that way took some restraint: a redesign is an easy moment to slip in a feature, and this release was meant to show that identity and voice can change without one.

0.5.0 shipped signed and notarized, and the app from the final DMG transcribed a local file, a YouTube link and a recording.
